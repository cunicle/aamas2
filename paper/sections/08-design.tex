\label{sec:design}

An agent in deployment must give every slot a length before it knows the value. We tested two ways to cope with this (Table~\ref{tab:mitigation}).

\paragraph{A bias toward closing.} Adding $\beta$ to the logits of the closing and padding tokens in every slot rescues large surpluses: with eight extra masks per slot, \dream's set accuracy at $k=4$ rises from 57.0\% to 72.3\% at $\beta=2$, against 87.0\% with exact lengths. It does not rescue near misses: with two extra masks, set accuracy stays at 2.8\% at $\beta=2$ and reaches only 13.8\% at $\beta=8$, a bias that empties many slots of the large surplus, where only 37.3\% of the output then parses. We chose $\beta$ on the evaluation requests, so 72.3\% is optimistic.

\paragraph{Lengths from the model.} We give every slot as many masks as its type allows, run one forward pass, read the probability of a closing token at each position as the hazard that the value ends there, and take the most likely length. CAL~\cite{liu2026cal}, which searches a single span's length with 11--18 extra forward passes, may be more accurate; we did not run it. Our estimate is exact for 51.6\% of \dream's slots and 72.8\% of \llada's, but all of a request's slots are exact in only 14.5\% and 27.0\% of the requests. With these lengths and one token per step, \dream reaches 42.3\% set accuracy and \llada 39.0\%, against 89.8\% and 92.0\% with exact lengths. \dream's wrong estimates are mostly too long (91.5\%, by a median of 7 tokens), where a closing bias helps (not run together); \llada's are as often too short, which no bias repairs.

\paragraph{Recommendations.} The interface writes the signals that the deciders read, the protocol decides who moves when, and the evaluation decides what is blamed.
(1)~\emph{Interface.} Right slot lengths assign roles and wrong ones reassign them, so avoid near misses: a slot one or two tokens too long is the worst choice, and a slot too short cannot be recovered. A closing delimiter written after the slot doubles the share of values that a near miss corrupts; writing it in the slot halves that share but costs accuracy unless the model closes reliably. Generous room with a closing bias reached 72.3\% in our tests, with the surplus counted from the true length.
(2)~\emph{Protocol.} A confidence threshold lets ordered calls move together (89.0\% with 1.9 forward passes per request) and makes symmetric calls move one at a time, because two symmetric choices are rarely confident at once (Section~\ref{sec:symmetric}). Teams of agents should take turns, or let one model fill a shared skeleton in which each call has its position; a number or a rule in the prompt does not keep simultaneous agents apart, and neither does sampling.
(3)~\emph{Evaluation.} Only the difference between parallel commits and one token per step, under the same controlled lengths, is a cost of parallelism (Section~\ref{sec:masquerade}).
