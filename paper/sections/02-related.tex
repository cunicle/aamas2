\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Tool-calling agents and parallel calls.} LLM agents interleave reasoning with tool calls~\cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer}, and many requests need several calls in one turn. LLMCompiler~\cite{kim2024llmcompiler} plans such calls and executes them in parallel, and BFCL~\cite{patil2025bfcl} evaluates parallel calls in dedicated categories. These works parallelize the execution of the calls; we study how one model generates them in parallel.

\paragraph{dLLM agents.} Masked diffusion language models~\cite{sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada,ye2025dream,bie2025llada2} are being tried as agent backbones. Lu et al.~\cite{lu2026bitter} find that dLLM agents fail at tool calling, often with malformed JSON or wrong parameters, and attribute this to diffusion noise and to parallel decoding that weakens causal dependencies; they do not vary slot lengths or the number of tokens committed per step. With matched fine-tuning, dLLM agents run over 30\% faster than AR agents but are more prone to structured tool-call failures~\cite{zhen2026dllmagent}. DLLM-Searcher~\cite{zhao2026dllmsearcher} has a dLLM search agent decode its tool call first and keep reasoning while the tool runs, and Qiu et al.~\cite{qiu2026reverse} trace the retry loops of dLLM agents to the sampler committing its most confident positions first. Agents of Diffusion~\cite{khanal2026agents} uses two AR LLM agents to steer a frozen dLLM toward schema-conformant data. We control the agent's action interface and decoding protocol separately.

\paragraph{Coordination without communication.} Players who cannot talk coordinate on focal points~\cite{schelling1960strategy,mehta1994salience} and conventions~\cite{lewis1969convention}; in dispersion games they must instead spread over the actions~\cite{grenager2002dispersion}; zero-shot coordination exploits known symmetries~\cite{hu2020otherplay}. LLMs often find focal points without communicating~\cite{aharon2026tacit}, coordinate well when the environment decides but less when they must model their partners~\cite{agashe2025llmcoordination}, and choose alike by default~\cite{ballestero2026monoculture}. Parallel LLM workers coordinate through a shared attention cache~\cite{rodionov2025hogwild} or a shared context and task queue that cuts redundant work~\cite{mao2026delm}. We apply these notions to the deciders inside one agent's decoding and compare them with teams of LLM agents.

\paragraph{Parallel decoding and length in dLLMs.} Tokens committed in the same step are sampled without their mutual dependencies~\cite{liu2025copula}. Samplers bound the resulting error with a confidence threshold~\cite{wu2026fastdllm} or an entropy bound~\cite{benhamu2025ebsampler}, and ParallelBench~\cite{kang2026parallelbench} documents invalid combinations such as ``New City''. A fixed canvas length is a known limitation: code infilling degrades when the number of masks does not match the completion~\cite{wu2026dreamon}, fixed format anchors impose rigid spans that truncate content or add redundant content~\cite{han2026dia}, and ample masks for schema fields lead to over-generation~\cite{xiong2026s3}. The remedies size a single span or response: CAL~\cite{liu2026cal} searches for a span's length, DreamOn lets the model grow or shrink its canvas, DIA estimates where a span ends, and $\rho$-EOS~\cite{yang2026rhoeos} and DAEDAL~\cite{li2026daedal} adjust a response from end-of-sequence signals. Constrained decoding for dLLMs enforces grammars~\cite{mundler2026cfg} or finite automata~\cite{dang2026constrained}, as it does for AR models~\cite{geng2023grammar}; our skeleton is the simplest such constraint. We do not claim to discover the length bias. We measure it in actions with several slots, show that it binds values to the wrong calls, and show that these errors pass for coordination failures of parallel decoding.
