  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
    \toprule
    Slot lengths & Call 1: institution, amount, income & Call 2: institution, amount, income & Verdict & What went wrong \\
    \midrule
    exact & \texttt{HSBC}, \texttt{500000}, \texttt{100000} & \texttt{Wells Fargo}, \texttt{700000}, \texttt{120000} & correct & --- \\

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{A dLLM agent's parallel turn under three slot lengths (a verbatim BFCL request). The interface writes the syntax in advance (a \emph{skeleton}) and leaves each value as a \emph{slot} of $\ell$ mask tokens, its slot length (Figure~\ref{fig:setup}a). \dream, a 7B diffusion LLM, fills the slots committing \emph{one token per step}, so no two decisions are ever made at once. Wrong parts in bold red; verdict of the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) checker. With swapped lengths, each call takes the bank whose name fits its slot: an error that looks like a coordination failure of parallel decoding, made without any parallel decoding.}
  \Description{A table of the agent's two calls under three slot lengths. With exact lengths both calls are correct. With one extra mask per slot, both bank names gain the word Bank and every amount gains a zero. With swapped lengths, the two bank names trade places between the calls.}
