If parallel decoding caused an agent's errors, we would expect particular ones: a value bound to the wrong call, a duplicated call, a value spliced from two calls. Wrong slot lengths produce the first two even with one token per step (Table~\ref{tab:masquerade}).

\paragraph{Swapped lengths.} With swapped lengths and one token per step, \dream has cross-call errors in 15.2\% of the swap requests, against 1.2\% at 16 tokens per step with exact lengths (Holm $p<10^{-4}$), all of them values bound to the wrong call. The rotated sibling group follows the length while the other arguments of the call follow the order, so one call's arguments name different entities (Figure~\ref{fig:example}).

\paragraph{What an evaluator sees.} With the lengths that the model estimates itself (Section~\ref{sec:design}), the condition closest to deployment, \dream's \ccer is 6.5\% with one token per step and 10.3\% with 16, against 1.0\% and 2.0\% with exact lengths. Both factors matter, and they interact: parallel commits add 1.0 point with exact lengths but 3.8 with estimated ones ($p=0.0026$; interaction 2.8 points, [0.3, 5.3]). Still, one token per step with estimated lengths has more cross-call errors than 16 tokens per step with exact lengths (difference 4.5 points, [2.0, 7.2], Holm $p=0.018$; \llada: 12.0\% against 3.0\%, not significant after correction). Surplus masks mostly corrupt single values (\dream's \ccer with one extra mask: 2.8\%, not significantly above 2.0\%), though with eight, \llada binds a value to the wrong call in 26.0\% of its requests. Splices are rare. With one token per step, \dream writes none in the conditions of Table~\ref{tab:masquerade} and 5 in the others, mostly garbled dates such as ``202021-01-01''; its clearest splices, such as ``San Alto'' from San Francisco and Palo Alto, appear at 4 and 16 tokens per step with wrong lengths, where wrong lengths and parallel commits act together.

\paragraph{An evaluation protocol.} These results concern skeleton interfaces; we do not claim that the errors reported for dLLMs elsewhere~\cite{lu2026bitter,kang2026parallelbench} come from slot lengths. But in a skeleton interface, an error that persists with one token per step is not an error of parallel decoding. Before attributing a dLLM agent's cross-call errors to parallel commits, an evaluator should decode the same requests with one token per step under the agent's own length policy and report the difference as the cost of parallelism in that interface; the errors that remain with one token per step should be reported separately; controlled lengths (exact, swapped, surplus) trace them.
