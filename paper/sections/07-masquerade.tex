The worry about parallel decoding predicts particular errors: a value bound to the wrong call, a duplicated call, a value spliced from two calls. This section shows that wrong slot lengths produce exactly these errors when the agent commits one token per step, where no two decisions are ever made at once.

\paragraph{Swapped lengths.} We rotate the slot lengths within each sibling group by one position, so that every changed slot gets the length of a sibling's value, and keep everything else fixed (Table~\ref{tab:swap}). With one token per step, 79.4\% of \dream's swapped slots then hold the sibling value whose length fits the slot, against none of the same slots with exact lengths. The slot does not merely lack room for its own value: when the swapped slot is longer than the slot's own value, so that the value would fit and the slot could close early, 79.1\% still take the longer sibling (79.7\% when it is shorter). Committing more tokens per step changes little (77.2\% at $k=4$, 73.3\% at $k=16$). \llada follows the length less often but still more often than its own value (47.5\% against 12.5\%, on 34 requests); the rest of its swapped slots are mostly truncated or other values. Set accuracy falls less than slot accuracy because a whole sibling group that rotates together can still match the reference set: in one request, \dream swapped both the artist and the play time of two calls, which is a correct set of calls in another order.

\paragraph{The length outranks the order.} A swap sets two cues against each other. The mention order points each slot to its own value, which is what the models follow when the cues agree (Section~\ref{sec:ordered}). The length points to a sibling's value. When they disagree, \dream follows the length about 15 times as often as the order (79.4\% against 5.4\%), and \llada about 4 times as often. In coordination terms, a signal written on the shared canvas overrides the focal point offered by the request.

\paragraph{What an evaluator sees.} Table~\ref{tab:masquerade} counts the cross-call errors of our taxonomy under two conditions on the same requests: the most parallel decoding with exact lengths, and one token per step with wrong lengths. In the setting closest to deployment, where the model predicts its own slot lengths (Section~\ref{sec:design}), one-token-per-step decoding has cross-call errors in 7.5\% of \dream's requests and 14.0\% of \llada's, against 2.0\% and 3.0\% at 16 tokens per step with exact lengths. Chimera values, which splice individually likely tokens of two values into one (ParallelBench's ``New City'' from ``New York'' and ``Mexico City'', which it attributes to parallel decoding~\cite{kang2026parallelbench}), appear one token per step: in 3.0\% of \dream's requests with one extra mask and 7.0\% of \llada's, against none at $k=16$. \llada writes ``HSBC Fargo'' into the first call of Figure~\ref{fig:example} with one extra mask. With eight extra masks, \llada duplicates a call in 11.0\% of its requests and binds a value to the wrong call in 26.0\%. One condition does not follow the pattern: \dream with eight extra masks has as few cross-call errors as with exact lengths (1.8\% against 2.0\%).

\paragraph{What the wrong actions would do.} If the agent executed its calls, wrong lengths would change what it does, not only how it is scored. With one extra mask, 33.3\% of \dream's turns would pass a number that is off by a factor of ten or more, against none with exact lengths. With swapped lengths, 13.9\% of the affected turns would act on another call's entity, against 1.0\% of all turns at $k=16$ with exact lengths. With its own length estimates and one token per step, \dream would pass a number off by a factor of ten in 19.8\% of its turns and act on another call's entity in 4.0\%.

\paragraph{An evaluation protocol.} An error that persists when the agent commits one token per step is not an error of parallel decoding. Before attributing a dLLM agent's cross-call errors to parallel commits, an evaluator should decode the same requests with one token per step and with controlled slot lengths (exact, swapped, and surplus), and attribute to parallelism only the difference that remains.
