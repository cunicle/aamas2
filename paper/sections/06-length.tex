We now turn to the interface. Sections~\ref{sec:ordered} and~\ref{sec:symmetric} gave every slot exactly as many masks as its reference value has tokens. An agent in deployment cannot do this, because it does not know the values before it decodes them.

\paragraph{One extra mask.} With one token per step, one extra mask per slot lowers \dream's set accuracy from 89.8\% to 15.5\% and \llada's from 92.0\% to 16.0\% (on its 100 requests; Figure~\ref{fig:surplus}a,b). The share of \dream's slots that hold their own value falls from 96.0\% to 37.5\%, and 44.4\% hold their own value followed by more (Table~\ref{tab:length}; Figure~\ref{fig:example}). Near misses hurt most: set accuracy is lowest with two extra masks (3.3\%) and recovers with eight (60.0\%; \llada: 38.0\%). With one or two masks left after the value, \dream gives a closing token only 1.0\% and 3.3\% of its probability (Figure~\ref{fig:surplus}c). It would rather write something else, but often not content: its most probable token at the first surplus position lies outside the slot's type mask in 59.3\% of slots, mostly an escaped line break in strings (1,076 of 1,464) and a type suffix after numbers; with two extra masks, 965 of 1,014 integer slots would write a decimal point (\$500{,}000.0). The type mask turns this filler into content. Format-tolerant slots also accept such tokens and remove them when decoding. They raise set accuracy with one and two extra masks to 28.3\% and 20.0\% (differences 12.8 and 16.8 points, both $p<10^{-4}$), and the share of slots that hold their own value with one extra mask from 37.5\% to 56.7\%, while leaving exact lengths almost unchanged (90.0\% against 89.8\%). Still, 24.8\% of the tolerant slots hold their own value followed by more content: the type mask explains a part of the damage, and the rest comes from the closing delimiter and the mask count.

\paragraph{The closing delimiter.} Ending a value early also means writing a closing token right before the one the skeleton has already written, a pattern rare in training. To separate this from the mask count, we move the closing token into the slot: the skeleton no longer writes it, the slot gets one more position, and after the model's closing token the slot is filled with spaces, which leaves ordinary JSON (Table~\ref{tab:length}, middle; dashed in Figure~\ref{fig:surplus}). For strings the delimiter explains almost all of the effect: with one extra mask, a string closes with probability 69.9\% instead of 0.5\%. Numbers still grow: an integer closes with 19.3\% at one extra mask, against 80.8\% when only the closing position is left. Overall, the share of slots that hold their own value falls from 89.9\% to 62.5\% instead of from 96.0\% to 37.5\%, and 26.2\% still overfill. The variant is no remedy as it stands: \dream often fails to close a value or closes it with a merged token such as \texttt{\}\}}, so 23.3\% of its outputs do not parse even with exact lengths and set accuracy drops to 70.3\%; we therefore read its slots one by one.

\paragraph{Swapped lengths and one slot longer.} Rotating the slot lengths within each sibling group gives every changed slot the length of a sibling's value. A slot made shorter can rarely hold its own value (5.1\% do, through a shorter accepted form); a slot made longer can, by closing early. With one token per step, \dream's lengthened slots take the sibling value that fits in 79.1\% of cases (95\% interval over requests [71.8, 85.7]) and keep their own in 5.8\%; with the closing token in the slot, 49.2\% [41.4, 57.0] and 15.5\%. In a swap, though, the sibling also gets a slot of the wrong length and may take the lengthened slot's value first. When only one slot of a group is lengthened and its sibling keeps an exact slot, the lengthened slot takes the sibling's value in 35.4\% of cases and keeps its own in 21.8\%; with the closing token in the slot, the order reverses (14.0\% and 40.3\%). So a sibling's length pulls a slot toward the sibling's value mainly when the delimiter makes closing early unusual, or when the sibling has itself been moved. The type mask plays no part here: with format-tolerant slots, the canvas is identical in 164 of the 165 swap requests.

\paragraph{Agents that make one call.} The cue needs no shared canvas. On the 165 swap requests, exact lengths, taken from the reference values, tell each agent which value is its own: anonymous \dream agents that act at the same time reach 48.5\% set accuracy (numbered ones 70.9\%), anonymous \qwen agents, which do not see the mask count, 0.6\% (difference 47.9 points, [40.0, 55.8]). With swapped lengths, 89.1\% of the swapped slots of \dream agents take the sibling value that fits (84.2\% when they take turns; Table~\ref{tab:length}).

\pred{3} holds in part. A slot too short mostly forces a different value, a slot with a sibling's length often takes it, and surplus masks are filled rather than left empty. How much damage this does depends on the interface: a pre-written closing delimiter keeps strings from ending early, and a type mask turns filler into content; removing either leaves part of the effect.
