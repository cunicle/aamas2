We now turn to the interface. Section~\ref{sec:ordered} gave every slot exactly as many masks as its reference value has tokens. An agent in deployment cannot do this, because it does not know the values before it decodes them. The simplest departure gives every slot $s$ extra masks; since the closing rule lets a value end anywhere in its slot, a model that knew where its values end would be unaffected.

\paragraph{One extra mask.} The models are far from unaffected (Figure~\ref{fig:surplus}a,b). With one token per step, one extra mask per slot lowers \dream's set accuracy from 89.8\% to 15.5\% and \llada's from 92.0\% to 16.0\%. The loss is not an artifact of scoring whole turns: the share of \dream's slots that hold their own value falls from 95.4\% to 34.0\%. On the same scale, committing 16 tokens per step instead of one costs \dream 5.3 points of set accuracy and 3.4 points of slot accuracy; one extra mask costs 74.3 and 61.4. Parallel commits make matters worse, not better: with 16 tokens per step and one extra mask, \dream keeps 5.0\% of its requests.

\paragraph{What fills the extra mask.} Most wrong slots keep the promise that the mask count makes. At $s=1$, 35.1\% of \dream's slots hold their own value followed by more, and only 0.5\% hold a sibling's value; the rest are other values (11.7\%), truncations (0.4\%), and output that does not parse (18.4\%). In the request of Figure~\ref{fig:example}, one extra mask turns HSBC into ``HSBC Bank'' and the loan of \$500{,}000 into one of \$5{,}000{,}000; four extra masks make it ``HSBC Holdings, Plc'' and \$5{,}000{,}000{,}000. A numeric slot cannot be lengthened harmlessly: every extra digit multiplies the amount by ten.

\paragraph{Near misses hurt most.} Set accuracy is not monotone in the surplus. For \dream it bottoms out at $s=2$ (3.3\%) and recovers to 60.0\% at $s=8$; \llada recovers to 38.0\%. The probe in Figure~\ref{fig:surplus}c shows why. We write the reference value into the slot, leave $s$ masks after it, and read the model's distribution for the next position. With one or two masks left, \dream gives a closing token only 1.0\% and 3.3\% of its probability; it is nearly certain that the value goes on. With eight masks left, it gives 74.0\%. \llada closes more readily (19.3\% at $s=1$, 91.8\% at $s=8$) but follows the same shape. A slot a little too long is read as a promise that the value is a little longer; a slot much too long is read as room to spare.

\paragraph{Padding is not the way out.} Our skeleton already lets the model write padding at any position of a slot, yet the models almost never pad inside a value: the median probability of padding right after the reference value is about $10^{-5}$ for \dream and below $10^{-5}$ for \llada at every $s$. S$^3$~\cite{xiong2026s3} reports the same over-generation when schema fields get ample masks, and prompts the model to fill unused fields with the word \texttt{null}. The closing token plays that role in our interface: it is the token that naturally ends a JSON value, and once it is written the rest of the slot is padded, so the model needs to take a single decision to stop. The probe measures exactly that decision. The AR agent, which stops at its closing token, keeps 89.0\% set accuracy with eight extra masks per slot, as the design guarantees.
